# CampusFix Problem Statement

## 1. Introduction

CampusFix is a proposed software system for reporting and managing university facilities maintenance problems. The system is intended to provide a more organized way for students and staff to report problems with university facilities and for those problems to be followed from reporting until resolution.

## 2. Who CampusFix Serves

CampusFix is intended to serve university students and staff who encounter problems with university facilities.

The system will also support maintenance personnel who are responsible for receiving, managing, and resolving reported problems.

The main users and stakeholders include:

- Students who report facility problems.
- University staff who report facility problems.
- Maintenance personnel who handle reported problems.
- University management or other responsible personnel who may need to monitor maintenance activities.

## 3. The Problem

University facilities can experience different types of faults and maintenance problems. Examples include:

- Broken computers.
- Faulty projectors.
- Lab lights that are not working.
- Water leaks.
- Faulty classroom doors.
- Other problems affecting university facilities.

At present, such problems may be reported informally. Informal reporting makes it difficult to keep track of reported faults and their progress.

It can therefore be difficult to determine:

- Which problems have been reported.
- Which problems are still unresolved.
- Which problems are urgent.
- Who is responsible for handling a particular problem.
- How long a user has been waiting for a problem to be resolved.
- Whether the same type of problem occurs repeatedly.
- How quickly maintenance personnel respond to reported problems.

## 4. The Gap

The main gap is the lack of an organized process for recording, tracking, prioritizing, and following up university facilities maintenance requests.

Without a centralized system, a reported problem may be difficult to track after it has been communicated. This can make it harder for responsible personnel to know which problems require attention and what stage each problem has reached.

CampusFix is intended to address this gap by providing a structured process for managing maintenance requests.

## 5. Proposed CampusFix Process

CampusFix will provide a process through which a student or staff member can report a facility problem.

The proposed process is:

1. A student or staff member reports a problem.
2. The system records the maintenance request.
3. The request is reviewed and prioritized.
4. Maintenance personnel receive the request.
5. Maintenance personnel handle the problem.
6. The status of the request is updated.
7. Once the problem has been resolved, the request is marked as resolved.
8. The request is then closed.

This process provides a way of following a maintenance problem from the initial report through to its resolution.

## 6. Project Goal

The goal of CampusFix is to develop a prototype system that provides an organized way of reporting and managing university facilities maintenance problems.

The system should help users report problems and help responsible personnel track maintenance requests, their priorities, statuses, responsibilities, waiting times, and resolution.

## 7. Expected Benefits

The proposed system is expected to provide a more structured approach to facilities maintenance reporting.

It should make it easier to:

- Record maintenance problems.
- Track unresolved problems.
- Identify urgent problems.
- Assign responsibility for handling requests.
- Monitor request status.
- Track waiting time.
- Identify recurring faults.
- Monitor maintenance response times.

## 8. Conclusion

CampusFix addresses the problem of informal and difficult-to-track university facilities maintenance reporting.

By providing a structured process from problem reporting to resolution and closure, the proposed system can provide a foundation for managing maintenance requests in a more organized and traceable manner.
